I started APP not just because I felt isolated as a new parent — but because postpartum care felt uncoordinated and inaccessible here. What I wanted was one trusted place of guidance, from both experts and fellow parents — so that's what **APP became: structure for new families navigating this unique, transformative season** of life.

Big mission, eh? So it's probably no surprise that…

## I started to break down

It was on a trip to the US that I realized I wanted to shift to building Amsterdam Parent Project (APP) full-time. I was visiting my in-laws — a wonderful, suburban, "American Dream" setup in the Northeast, similar to the one I grew up in. Surrounded by the trappings of my childhood culture, I realized two things:

- First: **My personal values had meaningfully diverged** from my family and friends back "home"
- Second: **I wasn't fully living by my values** here in Amsterdam

When I decided to take a new job in tech last February, I was at the tail end of parental burnout leave and building the APP community from a place of healing. I told myself: I can do both. I can build enterprise software with good people in an interesting domain, and fill my community cup with hyper-local impact through APP. I've always mixed full-time work and volunteering — I used to serve on the board of [SheSharp](https://shesharp.co) while working at [Trello](https://trello.com).

The difference this time around? I also had a growing toddler.

Working full-time and building APP on the side and parenting a 2 year old (and dog!) meant that my days looked like this:

![part-time](/static/images/behind-the-desk/going-full-time.png)

In the US, being busy is a badge of honor. The more packed your schedule is, the more "successful" you look. Success should mean money, and money buys luxuries that make life fuller.

**I left the US because the hustle mentality was burning me out. I carefully reintroduced work/life balance** — not just time-wise, but emotionally, culturally — here in Amsterdam. Then I lost it again when my daughter was born: desperate to reprove my worth, and to show everyone that I could "bounce back" as a working mom, as productive as ever.

But **my definition of "bouncing back" postpartum was somehow even more overextended than before**. Here I was with three full-time jobs — tech, nonprofit, parenting — stretched to the limit and burning out again 🫠

It took standing back in the life I decided to leave behind in the US 6 years ago to remember that moving to Amsterdam was the best decision I ever made — for my health, my family, and how I want to contribute to society.

I gave notice somewhat out of the blue, and about a month later, I left the tech industry behind.

## My community and tech halves, now as one whole

Faced with seemingly endless amounts of time, I felt the ideas flooding in. Then I looked at APP's plate and panicked: the programs were built around my limited capacity, on me showing up, not scaling up. **More time didn't mean more energy. What could/would/should APP look like if I actually gave myself room to breathe?**

I realized that the value I brought to the family support space looked really different from my peers — deeper than a social club or a birthing class. It was the foundation underneath those. **As a software engineer and product manager, I knew how to build infrastructure. As a builder for the community, I could do that in a way that wasn't mining data**, chasing AI features to appease investors, or adding more mental load to already-overwhelmed parents.

> Bespoke community software and **systems solving small, local problems** that new families in Amsterdam feel every day.

## What has changed since April

Since leaving, I've been doing just that: building software and systems like:

- [Groups Directory](/groups-directory): Discover local parent communities. 100+ local parent groups, 550+ parents, all in one (secure) place
- [Postpartum Post](https://postpartumpost.com): Skip the group chat, get to the deep stuff. Meet parents nearby with kids your child's age — 1:1 and on your schedule.
- Fourth Trimester Program &rarr; [First Year Program](/fyp): No more waiting for or missing a cohort. Rolling membership so that everyone with a baby under 1 can access support when they need it.

We're finally entering the "sustain, not build" era, and I couldn't be more excited. Next time, I'll share how these seemingly unrelated initiatives tie together — and how Amsterdam's postpartum care ecosystem is starting to rise.
